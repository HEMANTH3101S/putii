chapter six

A Promise

I'm not going to promise you the sky, because neither of us needs it — we already have the version of us that fits in ordinary days. What I can promise is smaller and truer than that. I'll keep showing up on the days that are hard for you and the days that are hard for me, even when showing up looks like nothing more than a phone call at midnight or sitting quietly next to you while you sort your thoughts. I'll keep learning you, Puttii, because I know people change and I don't ever want to be someone who stopped paying attention.

I'll tell you the truth, even when it's inconvenient, because I'd rather you trust me than think well of me for the wrong reasons. I'll be patient on the days you're not patient with yourself. I'll celebrate the small wins nobody else notices — the ones you almost don't mention because you think they're too small to matter. They're not. I'll fight for us instead of around us, which means I'll say the uncomfortable thing instead of letting it sit and rot between us.

I can't promise a life with no hard seasons. I can promise that whatever season we're in, I want to be in it with you and not just near you. That's the difference I care about. Pondati, I'm not choosing you today because it's easy — I'm choosing you today because every version of my life that has you in it is the one I'd pick again, on purpose, with my eyes open.

I'll keep choosing you.

chapter seven

Future

Maybe years from now...

...we're somewhere quiet, still figuring things out the way we always have, together instead of separately. Maybe there's a home with your books stacked wrong on my shelf and my coffee mugs in your cabinet. Maybe we're still arguing about the thermostat. Maybe there's a version of us that's a family, or maybe it's just the two of us and that's enough — I don't need the details to know the shape of it feels right.

What I want is simple: to keep growing next to you instead of apart from you. To still be the person you call first when something good happens, and the person you call first when something breaks. To build something ordinary and steady, because I think that's what love actually looks like once the beginning wears off — not fireworks every day, but someone who stays.
